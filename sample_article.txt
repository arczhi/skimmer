The Federal Reserve left interest rates unchanged on Wednesday but signaled that further hikes remain possible if inflation stays elevated.

The central bank's benchmark rate remains in a range of 5.25 to 5.5 percent, the highest level in more than two decades.

Officials noted that inflation has cooled considerably from its 2022 peak, but progress has been uneven across categories.

Shelter costs, which make up roughly a third of the consumer price index, continue to rise faster than the overall index.

Fed Chair Jerome Powell said the committee needs to see more evidence before concluding that inflation is on a durable path to 2 percent.

The economy has remained resilient despite the tightening cycle, with unemployment holding near 3.7 percent.

Consumer spending grew at an annualized 2.1 percent in the last quarter, slower than earlier in the year.

Markets initially rallied on the decision, with the S&P 500 gaining 0.8 percent before giving back most of the advance.

Bond yields ticked higher, with the 10-year Treasury note reaching 4.35 percent.

Powell acknowledged that the full effects of past rate increases may not yet have shown up in the data.

He also said the labor market would need to cool further to bring services inflation down.

Several officials have indicated they expect only one more hike this cycle, according to the latest projections.

Analysts remain divided on whether the Fed can achieve a soft landing, where inflation returns to target without a recession.

The next policy meeting is scheduled for September, and futures markets currently price in a roughly 40 percent chance of a hike.
